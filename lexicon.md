# Lexicon

A working glossary of terms this body of work uses in a particular sense —
coinages, borrowings, and words bent slightly out of their ordinary shape.
Definitions here are binding on the rest of the site. Where a term is
contested elsewhere, that is noted rather than resolved.

---

## Epigraphorism© { #epigraphorism }

**n.** A quotation set at the head of a work under an inverted citation format:
the writer's name first, the words after. Distinguished from the conventional
epigraph, in which the quotation leads and the attribution trails behind it in
MLA dress.

The inversion is the whole argument. Filing by writer rather than by utterance
concedes that a single writer may hold more than one, and that what is being
collected is *company* rather than ornament.

Coined in *The Bastard Son of the Great American Novelist*. See the
[Epigraph Concordance](epigraphs.md).

> The quotation, aphorism, axiom, truism, statement, couplet, accidental turn
> of phrase, lyric, utterance, or bit of wisdom.
>
> <cite>— the source's own list of what may qualify</cite>

## Nothos / Nothoi { #nothoi }

**n.** (Gk. νόθος, pl. νόθοι) In classical Athens, a child of a union the city
declined to recognise — most consequentially after Pericles' citizenship law of
451/0 BCE, which restricted citizenship to those born of two Athenian parents.
The *nothos* was not merely stigmatised; he was excluded from the *anchisteia*,
the circle of kin with rights of inheritance.

The term is load-bearing for this project because it names a legal condition
rather than a moral one. The bastard is not born wrong; he is *ruled* outside.
The English "bastard" carries a shame the Greek does not require.

## Relational Density { #relational-density }

**n.** A float between `0.0` and `1.0` carried in the frontmatter of every page
here, declaring how much of the work's argument passes through that page.

`1.0` — load-bearing; other entries depend on it.
`0.5` — substantive but self-contained.
`0.0` — inert; a holding pen.

The figure is a judgement, not a measurement. It is asserted by the writer and
is not derived from link counts. It exists so that the archive can be sorted by
consequence rather than by date.

---

## Variant { #variant }

**n.** Not a draft. A draft is a stage on the way to a finished thing; a variant
is a finished thing that happens to have siblings. *All The Collected Words* is a
variant, not a version.

The distinction matters because it determines what the repository is for. Version
control that treats earlier states as *superseded* would be the wrong instrument.
Here the commit history is an apparatus, not a landfill.
